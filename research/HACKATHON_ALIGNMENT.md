## Our review standard (proposed, not an official judging rubric)

For each problem statement, record:

1. **User and moment:** who is doing what, and where does work break today?
2. **Company knowledge:** which concrete organizational sources resolve it?
3. **Consequence:** what does missing, stale, or conflicting information cost?
4. **Desired outcome:** what can the person or agent do after using the product?
5. **HydraDB role:** which native capabilities deliver that outcome?
6. **RRFlow role:** which observable decisions need progressive evidence access?
7. **Demo:** one task, its evidence, the decision, a correction, and a reload.
8. **Measure:** expected answer or action, citations, failure cases, and latency.
9. **Scope:** what can be demonstrated with available data and remaining time?
10. **Distinctiveness:** what improves over a basic document-chat baseline?

Use the user's actual problem statements; do not substitute a generated one as
an approved direction. A public trend radar needs a concrete connection to an
organization's decisions to demonstrate this brief convincingly.

## UI direction to test with a chosen problem

Make the evidence journey visible: task and outcome, relevant knowledge avenues,
source inspection with freshness and provenance, observable tool activity, and
an explicit correction. Build loading, empty, missing-evidence, and error states
alongside the successful flow. A graph should help answer a task question;
visual novelty alone does not demonstrate organizational understanding.

Raindrop can help inspect and evaluate agent behavior. UI design and implementation
remain work for Codex and Claude. HydraDB remains the authoritative product state.
